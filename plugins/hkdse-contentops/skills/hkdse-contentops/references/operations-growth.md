## Positioning

Define one sentence:

`帮助[具体DSE人群]把[复杂问题]变成[可执行结果]。`

Default example:

`帮助普通话DSE学生和家长，把选科、备考和升学信息变成可执行清单。`

Use a teacher-led or education-team voice. Make the account valuable even before a user buys anything. Do not build the profile around exaggerated results or institutional authority the user has not supplied.

## Content pillars

Start with this adjustable mix:

- 30% subject choice and progression decisions;
- 30% subject study and diagnostic methods;
- 15% parent communication and陪考;
- 15% student misconceptions, questions, and emotional barriers;
- 10% livestream previews, FAQ cuts, and service explanation.

Build searchable clusters rather than isolated viral attempts. Each cluster needs:

- a broad search post;
- a diagnostic post;
- a practical checklist;
- a short-video explanation;
- a livestream or Q&A continuation.

## Publishing system

Recommend a sustainable starting cadence, not a platform guarantee:

- two graphic posts per week;
- two short videos per week;
- one livestream per week when staffing allows;
- one FAQ derivative from comments or the livestream.

For every calendar row provide:

- date or weekday;
- audience stage;
- topic and search phrase;
- format;
- funnel purpose: reach, save, trust, live attendance, or conversion;
- CTA;
- source/verification need;
- reuse plan.

Prioritize seasonal DSE stages. Do not attach a year to evergreen content merely to appear current.

## Acquisition funnel

Use this loop:

`search discovery → useful save → safe comment → related content → livestream → approved in-platform service → delivery → consented feedback → new FAQ content`

Design separate content for:

- cold users: clear search phrase and problem recognition;
- warm users: framework, comparison, and evidence;
- high-intent users: livestream diagnosis, service fit, scope, and limitations.

Do not treat raw views as the goal. Optimize for relevant saves, meaningful questions, livestream attendance, qualified platform-component actions, and retained trust.

## Weekly and monthly plans

For a weekly plan, return:

- weekly objective;
- four to six content items;
- one livestream topic;
- comment-mining question;
- one experiment;
- end-of-week review rule.

For a monthly plan, return:

- positioning and primary audience;
- four content pillars;
- four weekly campaign themes;
- publishing calendar;
- livestream sequence;
- acquisition path;
- measurement dashboard;
- next-month decision rules.

When the user supplies a brand, course, staffing, schedule, or past data, adapt the plan. Otherwise keep them as labelled assumptions.

## Diagnosis and experimentation

Ask for or inspect the smallest useful dataset:

- topic, format, publication date/time;
- impressions and cover click/read entry when available;
- graphic completion or video 3-second/average watch when available;
- saves, meaningful comments, follows;
- livestream entry, segment retention, comments, follows, approved-component actions;
- content and hook used.

Map the constraint:

- low discovery: search phrase, topic demand, account positioning;
- low entry: cover and title;
- low retention: hook, pacing, information density;
- low saves: insufficient action value;
- low comments: question too broad, unsafe, or demanding;
- low follows: weak account promise or no content cluster;
- low conversion: audience mismatch, insufficient trust, unclear offer fit, or unavailable platform path.

Run one-variable tests. Compare with the account's recent median and top-performing relevant assets. Do not present arbitrary universal benchmarks as platform rules.

After every review, decide explicitly:

- repeat: same audience problem, new angle;
- expand: turn comments into a cluster;
- revise: retain topic, change hook or structure;
- stop: low relevance or unacceptable factual/compliance risk.
